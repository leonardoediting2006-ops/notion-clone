# Dog Shorts formula (v2, from the analytics of Oct 7–26, 2026)

Read this before writing a new script. Each `scripts/<name>.md` follows it, and the "why this script" table at the
bottom of each file should cite the rule it uses.

## What the numbers say

Eight dog videos in 20 days made **99.8 %** of the channel's 178,592 views. The ~65 older videos (the August
"facts and scams" series, the 2024–25 glow-up clips) each got 1–54 views. Dogs are the channel now.

| Video | Dur. | Views | Avg view | % viewed | Subs / 1K views |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Toy (when they see you) | 27 s | 34.7K | 14.7 s | 55 % | 1.59 |
| Guilty look | 26 s | 32.4K | 12.8 s | 49 % | 0.99 |
| Eyes (evolved muscle) | 22 s | 28.8K | 10.8 s | 49 % | **0.24** |
| Bathroom | 25 s | 27.2K | 14.9 s | **60 %** | **1.88** |
| Stare (scientists explain) | 22 s | 24.1K | 11.6 s | 53 % | **0.21** |
| Tears (walk through the door) | 16 s | 23.3K | 9.1 s | 57 % | 1.38 |
| Leash (story) | 31 s | 4.1K | 12.6 s | 41 % | 2.43 |
| Bullet (story) | 27 s | 3.6K | 8.9 s | 33 % | 1.11 |

1. **"Why your dog does X when you…" explainers are the format.** 6 of 6 got 23–35K views (median 28K). The two
   third-person emotional stories got 3.6–4.1K. Do not make another story until the explainers stop working.
2. **Subscribers come from "you" moments, not from facts.** Toy and bathroom are 35 % of the views and **69 % of the
   subscribers** (106 of 153). Eyes and stare are 30 % of the views and 8 % of the subscribers (12 of 153).
   A video that explains a *biology fact* gets watched and forgotten; one about *what happens between you and
   him at the door* gets a follow.
3. **Viewers leave around 9–15 s, whatever the length** (16 s to 31 s videos all average 9–15 s). The payoff has to land
   before ~10 s, and the second half needs its own reason to stay. The seamless loop cannot help the half of the
   audience that never reaches the end; no video has reached 100 % viewed yet, so the loop has not shown a lift.
4. **Reach comes in 1–3 day bursts and then stops.** After day 4 a video gets 0–200 views a day. Daily views are 13–25K
   only on days a fresh video is in its burst; days with no new video fell to 0.04–3K (Oct 10, 18, 25, 26). A gap day costs
   roughly 10–20K views.
5. **Judge a video at 72 hours, not 24.** Three of the six peaked after day 0 (bathroom 1.3K on day 1 → 11.4K on
   day 2, guilty 3.5K → 18.5K, tears about 7.3K → 13.9K; the tears split is estimated from the daily totals).
6. **Thumbnails and titles do not drive these views.** Impressions were only 80–443 per video (1 % of the views or less), so
   99 % of the views come from the Shorts feed. A high CTR (eyes 4.3 %) says nothing about reach; the first seconds of
   the video decide it.
7. **Length is not a problem.** The 25–27 s videos averaged 31K, the 16–22 s ones 25K (n = 6, so this is weak evidence).
   There is no sign that shorter is better.

## The script formula

**Subject.** An everyday moment between *you* and *your dog* that every owner has seen and can test today
("…when you come home / when you yawn / when you leave / when you talk"). Use "your", "you", "him" in the first line.
Avoid pure animal-biology claims as the main idea ("evolved a muscle", "scientists explain").

**Length.** 24–27 s, about 85–95 words at a fast but clear pace (~3.5–3.8 words/s). One idea per sentence.

| Time | Beat | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1.5 s | **Hook** | A command or "your dog" line the viewer can check right now ("Watch your dog."). Hero's face is in the first frame. |
| 1.5–6 s | **The behaviour** | What you do → what he does. Relatable and visual. |
| 6–11 s | **Payoff #1** | The explanation, with one concrete number or surprise (21 of 29 dogs). **Must land before 11 s.** |
| 11–19 s | **Payoff #2: the twist about them** | The personal version (owner vs stranger). Give a second reason to stay, because ~half the audience has left by 12 s. |
| 19–25 s | **The feeling + loop** | One warm line, then the unfinished sentence that runs into the hook. |

**Voice and claims.** Hedge anything the science only half-supports ("probably", "may be"). The owner is never angry
with the dog (the guilty-look comment). Name the research, never "scientists say" with nothing behind it.

**Picture.** Cute cartoon hero with big glossy eyes, bright daytime, changing places for each idea (home, lab, park…)
but only one idea per place, smooth camera, one small label at a time, captions always on screen. First frame = last frame.

**Title / tags.** "Why your dog ___ when you ___" plus one 🐶 and at most a short bracket; no hashtags in the title.
The title helps search and "suggested" only, so don't spend time on A/B testing it.

**Publishing.** One video per day if possible, never more than two days without one. Judge each video at 72 h.

## Next topics (each uses a "you" moment; fact-check the study before scripting)

1. **Which way your dog wags his tail when he sees you.** Quaranta et al. 2007, Current Biology: right-biased wagging
   for people/dogs they like. Easy to animate, very visual.
2. **Dogs can tell how long you've been gone.** Rehn & Keeling 2011, Applied Animal Behaviour Science: greeting is
   more intense after 2 h than after 30 min. A sequel to the best "when you come home" videos.
3. **Why your dog sleeps on your dirty clothes.** Berns et al. 2015, Behavioural Processes: the dog brain's reward
   area responds to the scent of familiar people.
4. **Your dog needs your words *and* your tone.** Andics et al. 2016, Science: praise lights up the reward area only
   when the words and the tone both match. It also fits the "guilty look" audience.
5. **Why your dog tilts his head when you talk.** Fits the "when you talk to him" frame; find a solid study first.
