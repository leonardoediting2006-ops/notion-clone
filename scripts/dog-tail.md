Seamless-loop Short · ~20.7 s (the recorded voiceover) · 86 words · fast pace (~4.2 words/s) · 1080×1920, 60 fps · **toon style** (flat 2D people in a 3D cartoon world)

One simple fact, no "scientists tested…" opener: **a dog's tail swings more to his right when he's happy to see someone, and more to his left when he's nervous.**

## Fact-check (keep claims inside what was found)

- **Right when happy, left when wary.** Quaranta et al. 2007, *Current Biology* (30 pet dogs): the wag leaned to the dog's right when they saw their owner (strongest), also right (weaker) for a stranger and a cat, and to the left for an unfamiliar, dominant dog.
- **Other dogs notice.** Siniscalchi et al. 2013, *Current Biology* (43 dogs): dogs watching a video where only the tail moved had a faster heartbeat and more anxious behaviour when it wagged left, and stayed relaxed when it wagged right.
- **Wording:** "swings *more* to his right" (a bias, not every wag), "his" right (say it from behind the dog, or the left and right will confuse people), "gets a faster heartbeat" (not "gets scared"). Both studies are small and from the same research group, so don't say "proves", "all dogs", or "knows what you feel".
- The "you're my person" line is a warm metaphor, like "hugging you with his eyes" in `dog-stare`; keep the tone, don't put a science claim on it.
