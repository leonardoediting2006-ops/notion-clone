# Why your dog stares at you (it's not a treat)

Seamless-loop Short · ~77.6 s (the recorded voiceover, `Compound_Clip_10.srt`) · ~280 words · 1080×1920, 60 fps · **toon style** (flat 2D people in a 3D cartoon world, a 3D hero dog) · **40 shots in 9 different places**, each a slow eased camera move with a real cross-dissolve at the cut (the dramatic cuts are almost hard) · vivid, saturated colour · no on-screen text (only icons and the word captions).

**What this video is about, in one sentence:** when your dog locks eyes with you he is not begging; in wolves a stare is a threat, but dogs evolved a way to look at us that releases oxytocin in both of you, and it is his way of hugging you.

## Voiceover (the recorded one, word for word)

> Your dog isn't actually staring at you because they want a treat. When you're sitting on the couch and look up to find your dog across the room, locking eyes with you with that soft, unblinking gaze, you probably think they want dinner, need a walk, or are waiting for instructions. You see, they aren't asking for anything. They're giving you a hug without using their paws. In the animal kingdom, eye contact is deadly. For wolves, looking another creature directly in the eyes is a declaration of war, a challenge for dominance or the final signal before an ambush. Wild animals actively look away to survive. Dogs are the only species on Earth that rewrote their own biology to do the exact opposite. Over thousands of years beside our campfires, dogs evolved a specialized muscle above their eyes that wild wolves don't even have, developed solely to mimic human expressions and draw your gaze into theirs. When scientists tracked canine brain chemistry, they discovered that when a dog looks into their human's eyes, both of your brains release an identical flood of oxytocin, the exact neurological cascade that bonds a mother to her newborn child. A dog, a dog, or animal on the planet shares that biological loop with us. To your dog, making eye contact isn't looking. It's the sensory equivalent of reaching out and wrapping their body around your heart. They know they don't have words to comfort you, and they don't have arms to hold you when life gets heavy. So instead, they sit in the quiet, catch your eye, and use the only bridge evolution gave them to tell you that you are loved and you are never alone.

The last line runs into the first on the loop: *"…you are never alone. **Your dog isn't actually staring at you** because they want a treat."* The closing frame is the opening frame (the golden dog's face, eyes on the lens, camera still pulling back).

## Why it keeps people watching

- **A pattern interrupt every ~2 s:** 40 shots, 9 places (living room, 3D garden, wolf clearing, space, stage, campfire, lab, dream-space, mother and baby), new camera, new colour, new icon each time.
- **An arc:** curiosity (why is he staring?) → danger (wolves, war, ambush) → surprise (the muscle, the DNA) → proof (the lab, oxytocin) → a gut punch (no words, no arms, rain) → warm payoff (sunrise, bells) → the loop.
- **Each beat has something to look at:** every claim in the voiceover is drawn (a crossed-out treat, a rewrite of the DNA, two glass brains beating together, a heart wrapped in light).
- **The hook** is a close-up of the dog's eyes on the lens, then an immediate twist ("it's not a treat").
- **Sound** follows the picture: heartbeats that race in the wild, stay slow in the lab and are strong when the dog wraps your heart; real fire, rain, crickets, a rooster and birds; a real cat, cow, pig and sheep for the animals that orbit the Earth; a long hush before the sunrise.

## Fact-check (keep claims inside what is known)

- **Staring as a threat:** a direct stare is a threat or challenge signal in many animals, wolves and primates included, and gaze aversion is a common way to calm things down. "A declaration of war" is the narrator's drama; wolves also look at each other during ordinary social life.
- **The muscle:** a 2019 study (Kaminski et al., *PNAS*) found that dogs have a facial muscle that lifts the inner eyebrow ("puppy eyes") and that wolves lack it or have only a trace. Shelter dogs that make the expression more are adopted sooner. That it evolved "solely to mimic human expressions" is an interpretation; the safe wording is "it may have been favoured because it makes people respond".
- **Oxytocin:** a 2015 study (Nagasawa et al., *Science*) found that when dogs and owners gaze at each other, oxytocin rose in both, and that hand-raised wolves did not show the same loop. "An identical flood" and "the exact same cascade as mother and newborn" are overstated; oxytocin is involved in both bonds. "No other animal shares the loop" means *as far as we know*.
- **"The only species that rewrote its biology":** dogs were the first domesticated animal and changed a lot, but other domesticated animals changed too. Keep it as the narrator's line.
- **"They know they don't have words / arms":** poetry, not science.

## Build notes (toon style)

- `shorts/dog-gaze/` uses the toon toolkit (`js/toon.js`, `js/puppet.js`, `js/toonworld.js`, `js/herodog.js`, `js/outside.js`) and these modules: `kit.js` (icons that are always kept in frame, particle streams, the cross-dissolve), `room.js` (the flat), `wild.js` (the wolf clearing) and `worlds.js` (space with the Earth and the DNA, the night camp with a flickering fire, the lab with self-drawing monitors, a colourful stage, the dream-space with the glass heads and the oxytocin molecule).
- **Smoothness:** every shot is a pure function of time and of its shot number; at a cut the old shot is drawn once more into a render target and cross-faded over the new one for 0.4 s (0.14 s for the dramatic cuts).
- **Loop:** shot 40 ends on the exact camera, dog pose and light of shot 1 at time 0.
- **Sound: real recordings only** (60 sounds, lossless FLAC, ~440 cues): `python3 tools/forge-gaze-sfx.py` makes the new ones (fire, rain, crickets, cow, pig, sheep, cat, rooster, mouse clicks, typing); the rest come from the dog-doorway and dog-clothes libraries. Credits in `shorts/dog-gaze/sfx/CREDITS.md`.
- ~4,655 frames at 60 fps: render on a PC with a GPU, with `voiceover.wav` placed in `shorts/dog-gaze/`:
  `git pull`, then `npm run render -- shorts/dog-gaze --headed`.
